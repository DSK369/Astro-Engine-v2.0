# Plan 2 — Implementation of the KP System

**KP System (Krishnamurti Paddhati)** is a predictive system of Vedic astrology developed by **K. S. Krishnamurti**. Its main purpose is to make predictions more precise by giving special importance to **Nakshatras, Sub-Lords, house cusps, and ruling planets**.

## 🌟 KP Astrology — Main Features

* **1. Krishnamurti Paddhati (KP)**

  * Developed by **K. S. Krishnamurti**.
  * It is derived from Vedic astrology but uses a more precise and event-oriented approach.
  * Its central principle is essentially: **the Sub-Lord decides the result**.

* **2. Placidus House System**

  * Traditional Vedic astrology commonly uses whole-sign houses.
  * KP traditionally uses the **Placidus house system** for calculating house cusps.
  * Therefore, the exact **birth time** becomes very important.

* **3. House Cusps**

  * KP gives considerable importance to the exact degree of each house cusp.
  * The cusp represents the beginning point of a house.
  * For example, the **10th cusp** is particularly important for career, profession and status.

* **4. Nakshatras / Stars**

  * The 27 Nakshatras are fundamental to KP.
  * Every planet occupies a Nakshatra ruled by a particular planet.
  * KP considers the **Nakshatra Lord (Star Lord)** extremely important for determining what a planet is capable of delivering.

* **5. Sub-Lord**

  * This is one of the most distinctive features of KP.
  * Each Nakshatra is divided into **9 unequal Sub divisions**, called Subs.
  * Each division has a **Sub-Lord**.
  * The Sub-Lord is used to determine whether the promise of a house will actually materialize.
  * Simplified hierarchy:

    * **Planet → Star Lord → Sub-Lord**
    * Planet gives an indication.
    * Star Lord indicates the houses connected with the result.
    * **Sub-Lord determines the final outcome.**

* **6. Sub-Sub Lord**

  * KP can go even deeper into subdivisions.
  * A Sub is further divided into **Sub-Sub divisions**.
  * These can be used for finer timing and analysis.

* **7. KP Ayanamsa**

  * KP uses its own **KP Ayanamsa** for calculating planetary positions.
  * This can produce slightly different planetary degrees compared with other ayanamsas.

* **8. Ruling Planets (RPs)**

  * Ruling Planets are a major KP technique.
  * They are generally determined from factors such as:

    * Ascendant Star Lord
    * Ascendant Sub-Lord
    * Moon Star Lord
    * Moon Sub-Lord
    * Day Lord
  * Some implementations also consider planets occupying or aspecting relevant positions.
  * RPs are especially important for **event timing and rectification**.

* **9. Significators**

  * KP astrology uses **significators** to determine which planets are capable of delivering particular houses.
  * Instead of simply saying "Jupiter is good for marriage," KP asks:

    * Which houses does Jupiter signify?
    * Through which Star Lord?
    * Through which Sub-Lord?
  * This makes the analysis much more event-specific.

* **10. House Signification**

  * Houses are generally analyzed in terms of the event being asked about.
  * For example:

    * **1st** → self, personality, beginning
    * **2nd** → wealth, family, speech
    * **3rd** → communication, courage, siblings
    * **4th** → home, property, education, happiness
    * **5th** → children, romance, creativity, speculation
    * **6th** → service, employment, disease, debts, competition
    * **7th** → marriage, partnership
    * **8th** → obstacles, transformation, inheritance
    * **9th** → fortune, higher learning, long-distance travel
    * **10th** → career, profession, status
    * **11th** → gains, fulfillment of desires
    * **12th** → expenditure, loss, isolation, foreign residence

* **11. Positive and Negative House Groups**

  * KP frequently groups houses according to the event.
  * For **marriage**, for example, the important houses are commonly:

    * **2** → family formation
    * **7** → spouse/partnership
    * **11** → fulfillment
  * Houses such as **1, 6 and 10** can indicate resistance to or separation from marriage depending on the question and context.

* **12. Promise of the Chart**

  * KP emphasizes that an event must be **promised in the natal chart**.
  * Timing techniques cannot normally create an event that has no underlying promise.
  * Therefore, KP first asks **"Is it promised?"**, and then **"When will it happen?"**

* **13. Cusp Sub-Lord Theory**

  * This is one of the most important KP concepts.
  * The **Sub-Lord of a house cusp** is examined to determine the results of that house.
  * For example, for a career question, the **10th cusp Sub-Lord** receives special attention.

* **14. 6th, 10th and 11th for Employment**

  * These houses are particularly important for service/employment:

    * **6th** → service/job
    * **10th** → profession/work
    * **11th** → gains and fulfillment
  * The relevant cusp Sub-Lords and significators are analyzed.

* **15. 2nd, 7th and 11th for Marriage**

  * KP commonly uses:

    * **2nd** → family formation
    * **7th** → marriage/spouse
    * **11th** → fulfillment of relationship desire
  * The 7th cusp Sub-Lord is especially important.

* **16. 5th House for Love**

  * The 5th house is associated with romance, affection and love relationships.
  * For a love-marriage question, KP may examine combinations involving **5, 7 and 11**.

* **17. 4th House for Property**

  * Property and home-related questions involve the **4th house**.
  * Depending on the specific question, houses such as **2, 4, 11 and 12** can become important.
  * KP examines the relevant cusp Sub-Lords and significators.

* **18. 3rd, 9th and 12th for Travel**

  * Travel analysis may involve:

    * **3rd** → short journeys
    * **9th** → long-distance journeys
    * **12th** → foreign places/being away from birthplace
  * The exact combination depends on the type of travel being investigated.

* **19. Foreign Settlement**

  * For foreign residence/settlement, KP generally examines combinations involving houses such as **3, 9 and 12**, along with relevant residence/property indicators.
  * The distinction between **foreign travel** and **permanent settlement** is important.

* **20. Horary / Prashna**

  * KP has a powerful **Horary system**.
  * A person can ask a question even without having a reliable birth time.
  * A **KP Horary number** is traditionally selected within a prescribed range, and a chart is constructed for the question.

* **21. Birth-Time Rectification**

  * KP can be used to correct an uncertain birth time.
  * Ruling Planets and significant life events can be used to narrow down the correct Ascendant/cusp positions.

* **22. Event Timing**

  * KP uses planetary periods along with transit considerations to time events.
  * Important techniques include:

    * **Dasha**
    * **Bhukti**
    * **Antara**
    * **Transit**
    * **Ruling Planets**
  * The planets operating during the event should generally signify the relevant houses.

* **23. Vimshottari Dasha**

  * KP commonly uses the **Vimshottari Dasha** system.
  * The planetary periods are examined through their house significations rather than merely judging whether a planet is naturally benefic or malefic.

* **24. Transit**

  * Transits are used to refine timing.
  * Particular attention is given to transits of planets connected with the event and relevant significators.

* **25. Planetary Aspects**

  * KP does use planetary relationships/aspects, but its approach is different from systems that rely primarily on traditional aspect interpretation.
  * **Star Lord and Sub-Lord analysis generally receives greater emphasis.**

* **26. Retrograde Planets**

  * Retrogression is considered in KP analysis.
  * It can affect the way a planet signifies or delivers a result and is considered along with the relevant Star/Sub-Lord structure.

* **27. Rahu and Ketu**

  * The lunar nodes are especially important in KP.
  * They can act as **agents of other planets** connected with their sign, star and house relationships.
  * Therefore, Rahu/Ketu should not be interpreted simply as standalone planets.

* **28. No Simple "Good Planet / Bad Planet" Approach**

  * KP focuses heavily on **what houses a planet signifies**.
  * A naturally benefic planet can produce an undesirable result if it strongly signifies houses relevant to an unwanted outcome.
  * Likewise, a traditionally malefic planet can produce useful results if its significations support the event.

* **29. Event-Specific Analysis**

  * KP is highly question-oriented.
  * Instead of interpreting the entire horoscope in a generalized way, the astrologer identifies the **houses required for the particular event**.
  * Then the relevant significators and Sub-Lords are examined.

* **30. KP's Basic Working Formula**

  * A useful way to remember the system is:

  **Planet → Star Lord → Sub-Lord → Houses Signified → Event → Timing**

* **31. The Most Important KP Concepts to Learn First**

  * If you're learning KP systematically, prioritize these:

    1. **House cusps**
    2. **Nakshatras**
    3. **Star Lords**
    4. **Sub-Lords**
    5. **Cusp Sub-Lords**
    6. **House signification**
    7. **Significators**
    8. **Ruling Planets**
    9. **Vimshottari Dasha**
    10. **Transit-based timing**
    11. **KP Horary**
    12. **Birth-time rectification**

## 🧠 The core idea in one sentence

**In KP astrology, the planet operates through its Star Lord, while the Sub-Lord is given the decisive role in determining whether and how the relevant house result will actually materialize.**

If you're learning KP seriously, the next useful step is to learn **exactly how to calculate and interpret a planet's Significators → Star Lord → Sub-Lord → Cusp Sub-Lord**, with a worked horoscope example.
